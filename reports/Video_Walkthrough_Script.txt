03. Moving averages and stock exploration
The stock explorer combines the closing price, two moving averages, crossover markers, and reported trading volume. A twenty-day average uses the current observation and the nineteen previous trading observations. The fifty-day average uses the current observation and the previous forty-nine. Incomplete windows stay null. For Bajaj, the first complete twenty-day average is 2,415 point 53 rupees on January twenty-ninth, 2015. The first complete fifty-day average is 2,283 point 80 on March thirteenth. The assignment's Bajaj averages are rounded to two decimals, while cross-stock analytics keep full precision. Date filters are applied after calculating the historical windows, so moving a filter does not reset the averages. The explorer also offers candlesticks, daily price changes, drawdown, and a data export.

04. Signals and date lookup
A Buy event occurs when today's twenty-day average is above the fifty-day average, and yesterday's was at or below it. A Sell event uses the opposite crossing. Other days are Hold, including the initial warm-up period. Hold means no new crossing; it does not mean that an investor has no position or that the price has no trend. The raw Bajaj assignment output has twelve Buy events, eleven Sell events, and 866 Hold days. Its first Buy is May eighteenth, 2015, and its first Sell is August twenty-fourth. The date lookup returns Buy for June twenty-first, 2018. A date without a trading record returns no result rather than an invented Hold. The signal history also identifies opposite crossings within thirty calendar days, which helps assess repeated trading and potential costs.

05. Correcting the two bonus issues
Two corporate actions substantially change the interpretation of this dataset. Infosys had a one-for-one bonus issue with an ex-date of June fifteenth, 2015. TCS had a one-for-one bonus issue with an ex-date of May thirty-first, 2018. Issuer documentation supports both adjustments. Prices strictly before each event are divided by two; the event day and later observations remain unchanged. This removes misleading drops of approximately fifty percent. Infosys changes from a raw decline of 30 point 91 percent to an adjusted increase of 38 point 17 percent. TCS changes from a raw decline of 23 point 82 percent to an adjusted increase of 52 point 36 percent. Adjusting the full history also changes five dated signal outputs. TCS's latest raw Sell becomes an adjusted Buy from April twentieth. Original prices remain available for the assignment checks.

07. A cost-aware execution scenario
Crossover counts do not by themselves establish profitable trading. The execution scenario therefore starts with one hundred thousand rupees in cash and uses adjusted prices. A close-of-day signal can execute only at the next observed opening price. The scenario is long-only, allows fractional units, ignores an initial Sell while in cash, and liquidates any remaining position at the final close. Fees and slippage are adjustable assumptions. With a fee of ten basis points per transaction and zero slippage, Bajaj's simulated return is minus one point zero nine percent, although its full-period price increase is about ten percent. This illustrates how timing and costs can change an outcome. The chart and downloadable trade log expose the calculations. Dividends, actual brokerage, taxes, and market impact are not measured here. This historical scenario is an educational comparison.
